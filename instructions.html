Instructions Assignment

For this assignment, the goal was to write a clear, audience-appropriate set of instructions for a technical or procedural task. I chose to document the process of dialing in a shot of espresso — a precision-based skill that requires careful adjustment of multiple variables to achieve a balanced, repeatable result. The document demonstrates my ability to break down a complex process into logical, accessible steps with appropriate safety warnings and reference aids.

How To Dial a Shot of Espresso

Introduction

Espresso is a concentrated form of coffee served as the foundation of countless café drinks, from lattes to cappuccinos. After reading this guide, you will understand how to properly dial in a shot of espresso with consistency. Dialing in espresso is the process of adjusting the grind size, dose, yield, and extraction time to achieve a balanced extraction.

When done correctly, this process ensures that each shot delivers the ideal combination of sweetness and acidity without tasting sour or bitter.

⚠ Warning & Safety Notes

Hot Surfaces — The group head, portafilter, steam wand, and metal components can become extremely hot during operation. Use caution and avoid touching heated parts directly.

Pressurized System — The espresso machine operates under high pressure. Removing the portafilter while a shot is being extracted can cause hot water and coffee grounds to spray outward.

Equipment & Materials

  • Espresso machine (fully warmed and clean)
  • Burr grinder
  • Portafilter and basket (dry and clean)
  • Tamper (correct size for your espresso machine)
  • Digital scale
  • Timer
  • Fresh whole coffee beans (7–21 days after roast)
  • Filtered water

Quick Start

  • Start with a 1:2 brew ratio (18g of beans in : 36g espresso out)
  • Aim for 25–35 seconds extraction time
  • Adjust grind finer if the shot runs too fast; coarser if it runs too slow
  • Enjoy your dialed espresso shot

Dial-In Process

1

Prepare the Machine

Turn on the machine and allow it to warm up completely — approximately 10–15 minutes before use. Lock in the portafilter and run a blank shot to stabilize the temperature.

2

Weigh and Grind Coffee

Weigh and grind the dose precisely. Place the portafilter on the scale, tare the scale, and grind 18 grams of coffee beans — the standard baseline starting point.

3

Distribute and Tamp the Grounds

Distribute the grounds evenly in the portafilter. Tamp firmly and level — keep your wrist straight and apply consistent downward pressure. Avoid tilting, as this causes channeling.

4

Begin Extraction

Insert and lock the portafilter into the machine. Place your tared cup on the scale beneath the spout. Start the extraction and your timer simultaneously when you hear the pump engage.

5

Observe and Track

Stop the shot at 36 grams yield. Confirm total extraction time falls between 25–35 seconds. The espresso stream should resemble a syrup-like flow — avoid pale or spraying streams.

6

Taste and Evaluate

Stir before tasting. Identify body, acidity, and bitterness. Determine if the shot is balanced, over-extracted, or under-extracted using the reference table below.

ObservationLikely CauseAdjustment
<25 sec, sour tasteUnder-ExtractionGrind Finer
>35 sec, bitter tasteOver-ExtractionGrind Coarser
Weak bodyLow ExtractionIncrease Dose Slightly
Too intenseHigh ExtractionIncrease Yield
7

Adjust and Repeat

Change only one variable at a time and repeat the process. Record each attempt — noting dose, yield, time, grind size, and tasting notes — until you achieve a consistently balanced shot.

Glossary

DoseAmount of dry coffee grounds used, measured in grams.
YieldAmount of liquid espresso output, measured in grams.
Brew RatioThe relationship between dose and yield (e.g., 1:2 = 18g in, 36g out).
Extraction TimeTotal time water flows through the coffee grounds.
ChannelingUneven water flow through weak spots in the coffee puck, typically caused by uneven tamping.
TampThe act of compacting coffee grounds in the portafilter.
PortafilterThe handle-mounted basket that holds coffee grounds during extraction.
Under-ExtractionSour, thin flavor caused by insufficient contact between water and grounds.
Over-ExtractionBitter, dry flavor caused by excessive extraction of compounds from the grounds.

Conclusion

Dialing in a shot of espresso is a deliberate and repeatable process built on precision, observation, and taste. By warming the machine, weighing the dose accurately, tamping evenly, measuring yield carefully, and adjusting the grind intentionally, you create the conditions for a balanced extraction.

Each step builds on the previous, and consistency is key. Remember that espresso is dynamic — each coffee bean is different. For this reason, dialing in espresso is not a one-time task but an ongoing practice. Change only one variable at a time, measure every shot, taste honestly, and enjoy your espresso.
