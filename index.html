About Me

Who I Am

I'm Nathan Duong, a Computer Science student at the University of Central Florida (Class of 2028) with a 3.8 GPA and a drive to solve real-world problems through software. My coursework in Computer Science I, Computer Organization and Logic, and Object-Oriented Programming has given me a strong foundation in both the theory and practice of building reliable, efficient systems.

Outside the classroom, I've applied these skills in fast-paced environments. At the STEM Connect Hackathon, my team and I built Hyve — a full-stack networking platform using React, Tailwind, and Supabase — in just 36 hours. That experience sharpened my ability to ship quickly without sacrificing code quality.

My professional background spans audio-visual production (live events, conferences, 50–100 attendees) and four years as a receptionist at Encore Nails & Spa, where I managed 70–80 guests per shift, handled multi-line phones, and processed financial transactions. These roles built the time management, communication, and adaptability I bring to every engineering team.

I'm also an active member of SASE (Society of Asian Scientists and Engineers) at UCF, where I engage with industry professionals and build community in STEM. I'm bilingual in English (Advanced) and Vietnamese (Conversational), and I'm actively seeking internship opportunities where I can grow and contribute.

Reflection

My Portfolio Journey

Building this digital portfolio has been both a creative and introspective experience. Overall, I'm satisfied with how it has come together — the clean, minimal layout reflects my values as a developer who prioritizes clarity and usability. The blue accent color gives it a professional, tech-forward feel without being overwhelming, and the section-based navigation makes it simple for any visitor — whether a recruiter or professor — to find what they need quickly. The fonts are intentionally easy to read, with generous spacing between paragraphs to reduce visual fatigue. One ongoing challenge has been deciding how much content to include. I want the site to feel complete and polished, but not cluttered — especially as I continue building projects and gaining experience to add over time.

Through this process, I've learned far more than just HTML and CSS. Writing my "About Me" section pushed me to think critically about which experiences actually make me stand out as a candidate — not just what I've done, but what skills each role built. I also found it challenging to present academic assignments in a way that feels professional and portfolio-worthy, rather than just submitted work. Questions I still have include how to best incorporate a downloadable PDF résumé, and how to keep the site current as I grow. Going forward, I plan to use resources like MDN Web Docs for design guidance, peer feedback from classmates, and inspiration from professional portfolios on GitHub Pages and Behance to continue refining this site throughout my academic career.
